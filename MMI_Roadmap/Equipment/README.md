# Equipment

**Status:** `PLANNING — no equipment purchased`
**Needed by:** Practical evaluation (target April 2027) / first certification under Schedule A

Provers are the single largest capital expense in MMI's certification plan after the courses themselves — and the only one where buying the wrong item means owning equipment that **cannot legally be used to certify anything**.

---

## The Rule That Governs Everything Here

Measurement Canada evaluates and publishes a list of test equipment recognized for inspecting retail fuel dispensers. Equipment other than closed-loop has been evaluated for **vapour loss effects when used on gasoline** — which is why an ordinary open-neck test measure is not acceptable for gasoline certification.

Two separate hurdles, both mandatory:

1. The model must appear on MC's **Recognized Retail Fuel Dispenser Test Equipment** list
2. The individual unit must be **verified and designated as a local standard** by Measurement Canada — see [Directive V-24](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/measurement-canada/en/laws-and-requirements/v-24-relevant-local-standards-volume)

> An unrecognized measure is an expensive stainless bucket. Confirm both before any money moves.

## Test Volume Required, by Dispenser Flow Rate

| Dispenser rated flow | Standard required |
|---|---|
| ≤ 20 L/min | 5 L test measure |
| ≤ 90 L/min | **20 L test measure or prover** (MC policy: test volumes not less than 20 L) |
| > 90 L/min (high-speed) | 250 L prover |

Ordinary retail gasoline and diesel sits in the middle row. That is MMI's starting scope.

## The Buying Decision — E-VRTM vs VEP

Both are recognized. Different form factors, different workdays.

| | **E-VRTM 20L + funnel** | **VEP Prover** |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Hand-carried test measure | 20 L standard on a holding tank |
| Cost | Lower | Higher |
| Vehicle | Fits any van, stows upright | Bulkier — low load floor or ramp |
| Product return | Carry ~15 kg of fuel back to the fill riser, **once per nozzle** | Holding tank keeps product on the cart |
| Best for | Starting out, small sites | Sites with many nozzles |

**How to decide:** get quotes on both, then work out the per-site time difference across a realistic day (a station has 8–24 nozzles). If the holding tank turns a full-day site into a half-day site, it pays for itself in billable sites per week. This is a revenue calculation, not an equipment preference.

## How Many Measures?

**One recognized 20 L is enough to start.** Reasons to add a second, in order of seriousness:

1. **Business continuity.** A local standard must be periodically re-verified by MC, and MMI cannot certify anything while it is away. A dent also takes it out of service — a dent changes the volume. One measure is a single point of failure for all inspection revenue. Buy the second *before* it is needed, not after.
2. **DEF, if ever in scope.** DEF equipment must be dedicated and never shared with other fluids. Non-negotiable separation.
3. **Throughput.** Standard procedure allows a 30-second drain period after cessation of flow, on top of pour-back and repositioning. A second measure lets one drain while the other fills.

Gasoline-vs-diesel cross-contamination is **not** a documented reason for separate measures — proper draining handles it.

> Build the van's shelving for three. Buy one.

## Vehicle

The provers size the vehicle. See the reasoning in full context, but the short form:

| Scope | Vehicle |
|---|---|
| Retail dispenser certification (20 L class) | **Used ¾-ton cargo van, mid-roof** — Ford Transit 250, RAM ProMaster 2500, Chevy Express 2500 |
| Bulk plant / vehicle tank meters (250 L class) | Cart, liftgate or trailer |
| NAWDS scales | Test weights are heavy — liftgate |

Requirements: lockable, dry, provers carried **upright and secured** (they are legal standards — dents invalidate them), low load floor for lifting ~30–35 kg full, shelving for seals, lead-wire crimper, certified thermometer, hydrometer, tools, laptop and printer.

**Keep registered gross weight at or under 4,500 kg** — above that triggers [CVOR](https://www.ontario.ca/page/commercial-vehicle-operators-registration-cvor) with annual inspections, logbooks and a safety rating to maintain. A Transit 250 or ProMaster 2500 sits well under. A trailer over 2,800 kg counts toward the total.

**Timing:** no vehicle is needed until Schedule A listing (target 2027). During the 1,000 PM.1 field hours the work happens in the host company's truck. Buying earlier is dead money.

Also required alongside the vehicle:
- [ ] Commercial auto insurance — a personal policy will not cover business use, and a denied claim with provers aboard is company-ending
- [ ] Fire extinguisher and spill kit — live forecourt work; drained provers still hold vapour
- [ ] **TDG status confirmed** — the [150 kg gross exemption](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/legisl/tdg/tdg_special_case.html) is used by many service vehicles, but is conditioned on containment "available to the general public at a retail outlet" weighing ≤30 kg. A stainless test measure with residual gasoline is not obviously that. **Confirm with Transport Canada or MC — do not assume.**

- [ ] Confirm with MC that the recognized-equipment list is **current** (published version is dated 2017)

- [ ] Quote: Seraphin VEP Prover

- [ ] Ask each supplier about used or demo units
- [ ] Confirm recurring MC verification cost per standard — this is an **ongoing** cost, not one-time

### Purchase
- [ ] 20 L recognized measure/prover purchased — **funnel model number on the PO if E-VRTM**

- [ ] Certified thermometer
- [ ] Hydrometer (density for VCF)
- [ ] Security seals and lead-wire crimper
- [ ] Certificate printer / field laptop

### After purchase
- [ ] Unit submitted to MC for verification and designation as a local standard
- [ ] Designation certificate filed → `Documents/`
- [ ] Re-verification interval diarized
- [ ] Equipment added to business insurance schedule
